# Steve v0.2.109

- Steve no longer masks, filters or refuses content. What you type is stored and routed as typed: the paste guard that replaced passwords, tokens and keys in messages, queued edits and config drafts is gone. What a model or a command says reaches you and the agent as it is. Session records, tool results, harness stderr, secret-use and Node command output, git push output, CI logs, GitHub comments and pull requests, knowledge, flow steps and delivered reports are no longer redacted.
- Channel replies, mail and GitHub pushes are no longer held back for containing a secret. The disclosure check no longer treats a secret value as a leak, but it still checks knowledge levels and audiences. A secret-use command with a token written into it now runs. Vault items, their encryption, approvals and grants are unchanged. If a model repeats a secret, switch to another harness or model.

- The Automation page leads with what needs you. The line under the title shows today's signals, how many need you and how many failed. A notice names a flow that failed today, a schedule whose last run failed, or an account that must be authorised again. With nothing set up yet, the Flows tab shows three steps to start: connect an account, create a flow, create a schedule.
- Flows take one line each, with their description or trigger in words (not event keys such as `github.check_failed`) and when they last ran or how often they failed today. Schedules show their time and next run, with a "Last run failed" pill instead of the whole error. Opening a schedule shows the error first. In the New schedule dialog, the missed-run and overlap options moved under Advanced. Event sources list connected ones first, and a source without an account shows one prompt to add one instead of a page of disabled switches.
- Signals no longer repeats per-outcome counts above the groups, and long reasons fold until "Show all". When nothing is connected yet, it shows how to connect an account. When nothing arrived today, it offers the last 7 days.
- The flow editor is a canvas. Steps run top to bottom as cards that say what they do, with branches as named outputs. Add a step from the list, by dragging it onto the canvas or with the + under a free branch. Drag from an output to connect two steps, and select a connection and press Delete to remove it. The canvas won't make a loop, and ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z) undoes. A step nothing leads to is marked Not connected, and Save waits until it is joined in. The canvas pans, zooms and works with the keyboard alone. Step positions are kept per device, and the flow is laid out again when Steve edits it or you roll it back.
- Dry run now replays the finished run on the canvas along the path it took. A run panel under the canvas lists the flow's recent runs, and opening one replays its path or shows its steps, timeline, event and failure.
- Connections (连接) is a directory of the services Steve can connect. Each card says whether it is connected, what it can do today and whether a connection needs attention, such as missing permissions or an expired authorisation. You can search, filter by connected and browse by capability.
- A connection on an account page is one settings card. Its switch pauses or resumes receiving, and its sections show problems with a re-authorise button, the capabilities Steve may use and the channel identity. For a mailbox, the send delay, archiving and sync range save at once. Its signature and duty save from a save bar right under them, and the browser asks before you leave with unsaved edits.
- Chats opens with what needs you. If an account stopped working or a new group has not been set up, a notice at the top names it and links to it. Each group or DM row now says what Steve does there, for example "Steve replies directly", "Drafts for you, you approve" or "Quiet: record only", with the time of the last message on the right. The provider and member count are no longer repeated on every row. One account filter replaces the separate source and account filters. Account rows show their provider, whom they speak as and how many chats they cover, and show a status only when something is wrong.
- A group's page starts with Steve's part there. The attention setting, role, record-only switch, persona and speaking identity sit in one card. If replies are waiting for your approval, a notice says how many and links to the inbox. The latest five messages come next, with "All N" for the rest. Duty and response rules follow. Knowledge and memory, item defaults, collaboration with other bots and members are folded under "More settings". The two persona rows are now one, and the speaking identity only shows when there is more than one to choose from.
- An account's page lists its groups and DMs first, each with what Steve does there and its duty on one line. A broken connection is a notice at the top with the error and a re-authorize button, and Check sits next to "Account and authorization". The default persona, rules and undo window are one section. Trigger words show only their switch until you turn them on. After a permission check, only missing permissions are listed. Granted ones fold into one line, and each scope ID shows inline instead of behind its own disclosure.
- A persona's page says where the persona is used at the top, instead of repeating its introduction, and lists those groups before the form.
- Connections: when something needs attention, a notice at the top names the service, the app or mailbox, and what is wrong. Only the problem part of a card's line is highlighted. With a handful of services, the page shows them without search, filter or categories. A service that is not connected shows how to connect first, then what it can do. Planned capabilities are one line, and the empty "Connected · 0" box is gone. A connected service shows its installations first, with "Connect another" folded below. The Feishu "authorize yourself" card is hidden once you have done so, and an identity repeats its status only when it differs from the card's.
- Mail: the mailbox filter is a menu next to the search, so the list has one row of controls. A draft waiting for your approval comes first on a thread row. On a thread, "Done" is a button in the header instead of a menu item.
- The wording is now "connect" everywhere on these pages: Connections, the mail and Feishu dialogs, and the automation guide. Chat pages say "account" instead of "channel account".
- Signals: the steps under "How it was handled" show each node's title instead of its ID. On a phone, a flow that failed today shows the count under its description instead of in a pill beside it.

- The Bot list shows each Bot as a card: its avatar, name and role, then its harness, model, running items and memories. Click the card to open the Bot. The switch and the ⋯ menu on the card still work on their own.
- A Bot's page opens on an Overview that is useful even when the Bot is idle. It shows who the Bot is, its last 30 days (turns, items done, first-pass rate, average time and cost per item, cache hit rate), its items with running ones first, and a summary of its capabilities, permissions, acceptance, system prompt and memories, each leading to the tab where you change it. A Bot that has done no work yet shows a short "Start configuring" guide instead.
- On the System prompt tab, "Tell Steve how to change it…" drafts a new version of the working rules or of your own prompt and shows it as a before/after diff. Nothing is saved until you press Apply. For integrations, `POST /api/config/draft` takes an optional `field` (`rules` or `prompt`) and `base` on a `bot` target, and the draft's `patch` carries the whole new text.
- In a Bot's Settings, the permission mode and the acceptance mode each choose from tiles with a plain-words description. The New Bot dialog uses the same tiles. In every mode, anything a Bot sends out through Steve (mail, GitHub actions) waits for your approval. The Memory tab uses the Knowledge page's memory panel, and the Schedules and flows tab lists the schedules the Bot runs.
- Codex Bots can pick a model before the Node has reported Codex's own list. Codex declares a built-in list (gpt-6.1-sol, gpt-6-sol, gpt-6-luna, gpt-5.6-luna), which is replaced by the adapter's report once the Node has probed it.
- **Capabilities** (能力) in the sidebar is the workspace's library of MCP servers, skills and remote agents. Each entry is defined once and chosen per Bot. The tabs are MCP servers, Skills and A2A, and every entry shows which Bots use it. A Bot's **Capabilities** tab lists what it uses: Steve · built-in first (always on, its tools folded inside with how often this Bot called each in the last 30 days), then its MCP servers, skills and remote agents, each with a switch for that Bot. **Add** picks one from the library or makes a new one. Editing an entry changes it for every Bot that uses it.
- MCP servers are a local command (stdio) or an HTTP or SSE endpoint, with environment variables or headers. A Bot's sessions get the servers it has switched on through ACP, next to Steve's own tools, the same way for every harness and Node. A change applies the next time a session's harness opens. A variable or header marked secret goes into the vault. It is never shown again, stored with the server, logged or put in the Node's command or journal, and the Node takes it from the Hub only when the harness opens.
- Skills are `SKILL.md` instructions, written where the Bot's harness reads skills before a session opens. An adapter declares whether and where it reads skills (`skills`); Claude Code and Codex do. A new skill can be filled in from a pasted `SKILL.md`.
- Remote agents (A2A) are added by their Agent Card address (a bare host is looked up at `/.well-known/agent-card.json`), and their name, description and skills are shown before you keep them. A key the agent needs goes into the vault. A Bot that uses one gets a tool, `steve_a2a_<name>`, that sends the agent a task and brings back its answer and artifacts. If the agent is still working after 100 seconds, the tool hands back the task id so the Bot can ask again later.
- Steve from outside: Claude Desktop, Cursor or another agent can connect to the Hub's `/mcp` with one of your access tokens (`Authorization: Bearer stt_…`), also from another machine. It gets the tools that need no Steve session: find and read items, hand Steve new work (`steve_item_create`), add to an item (`steve_item_supplement`), the knowledge base, your memories (read only) and the session list. **Capabilities · MCP servers** shows the address, makes a token (shown once) and gives a configuration to copy for a generic MCP client, Claude Desktop, Cursor and Claude Code.
- Steve as an A2A agent: its Agent Card is at `/.well-known/agent-card.json`, and `POST /a2a` with an access token takes `message/send` (a new task becomes an item, and a task id adds to that item), `tasks/get` and `tasks/cancel`.
- API: `/api/capabilities/{mcp-servers,skills,a2a-agents}` (with `POST …/a2a-agents/preview`), `GET /api/capabilities/steve`, `GET /api/bots/{id}/capabilities` and `PUT|DELETE /api/bots/{id}/capabilities/{mcp|skill|a2a}/{entry}`. Of the migrations that run on upgrade, 65 adds the library (`mcp_servers`, `skills`, `a2a_agents`, `bot_capabilities`) and drops the per-Bot tables that 62 and 63 create on the way. See "Capabilities" in `docs/deployment.md`.
- A Bot asked to release in a project without a release action gets an error telling it to deliver instead. Before, it waited for a release that never came.

- Steve's questions and decisions are a new card, shown in the conversation, in Pending and on an item's page. The question comes first, then numbered choices: click one or press its number. The last row takes your own words (an answer, what to rework, a reason to reject, a note). Every answer offered before is still there. Decisions that need their full view, such as mail drafts, contacts and flows, offer a row that opens them.
- A tool permission is a card of its own. It shows the command and its arguments, with Deny and Allow once and, where the agent offers it, a checkbox to allow that kind of call from now on. ⌘↵ allows while the card has focus, and denying always takes the Deny button.
- The line above the main composer that counted waiting decisions is now that same card, folded to one line. Open it to answer there and page through them. A decision the conversation already shows in place is not repeated.
- "While you were away" is a compact card: when you left, and what waits on you, failed or got done since, with one action per row. Items still running are left out, because the side panel shows them.
- The work behind an answer reads as one timeline, in Steve's conversation and in an agent's run alike. A step's verb follows its state ("Reading" becomes "Read 5 files"). Calls of one kind in a row merge into one line with their subjects as chips. Thinking streams in with a clock and folds to "Thought for 6s". A plan checks its steps off, and handing work to a subagent reads "Delegated to …". Steve's own tools go by what they do ("Read item", "Search knowledge") instead of their internal names.
- The composers in the main conversation, on an item's page and in a session share one design. Files, an item reference (#), handing to a Bot (@) and, in the main conversation, the commands (新事项, 新项目, 汇报, 新 Bot) are in a + menu. Typing #, @ or / still opens them. While Steve answers in the main conversation, Stop stays available and what you send queues.
- The actions under Steve's replies (Copy, Quote reply, Make an item, Answer here instead) are a quieter bar with tooltips. While Steve works, its current phase shows next to a small spinner.
- Repeated notices, such as the same disk warning again, fold into one line. Bold text in Chinese replies renders even when it ends on a full-width mark, and quote lines show words instead of Markdown marks.

- Collaboration is planned to finish sooner. Steve splits work into parts that can run at the same time instead of a chain of phases, keeps resource keys per module or path, and does single-sequence work in its own session. A failed review goes back into the unit it found problems in, and that unit is retried instead of fix units being added to the end. When a plan leaves out `max_parallel`, every ready unit whose resources are free runs. A plan that cannot run anything at once, independent units that share a resource key, or a limit below what the graph allows come back as notes in the plan reply. They are hints and never refuse the plan.
- When a model's quota runs out and its error says when it is back (a 429 with "available again at 00:40 UTC"), the item waits until then and continues the agent once the quota is back. Before, Steve continued at once, failed again within a second and used up the continues.
- A Steve tool result too large for one Node message (over about 256 KB) reaches the agent cut, with a note of how much was cut and how to ask for the rest, instead of failing the call. A message that is still too large names the call it belonged to.
- A Node that reaches the Hub but cannot link now tells the Hub why. Devices shows the reason while that Node is disconnected (for remote Nodes and the Hub's own), and work waiting for the Node says why it waits. A Node that cannot reach the Hub at all records the reason in its own log and `link-status.json`.
- With remote Nodes off (`STEVE_NODE_LISTEN=0`, or a read-only copy), the Devices page says so instead of failing, and the Node API answers in JSON.
- A web client on another origin that the Hub does not let in yet shows how to sign in by token, instead of "Can't reach Steve". Signing in by token lets that origin in, and the browser sign-in, which could not return there, is no longer offered on that screen.
- Smaller fixes: YOLO's answers to tool calls read "allow this once" instead of an option id; the locked vault no longer suggests creating an item it can't create; GitHub shows a connection that needs reauthorisation as "Needs reauthorization"; modules that are not built yet say they are planned for a later release; the mail page's connect button opens the mailbox setup under Connections; a session's header keeps its way back visible on narrow windows.
- For contributors: `python3 ops/dev.py` runs a checkout as a development Hub next to the installed one, with the web dev server in front, so a change can be tried in seconds without CI or a release. `up --fresh` starts from an empty database. `up --snapshot` starts from a copy of the installed Hub's data that shows it without acting on it (nothing resumes, no Node, vault locked). `restart` rebuilds the Hub incrementally. The installed Hub's pages, the web dev servers and the origins in `STEVE_DEV_CLIENT_ORIGINS` may call the development Hub directly. See CONTRIBUTING.md.

- The item page leads with one state: what is happening and whose move it is. It sits in a "now" card that holds the decisions, blockers and workflow actions, and says why the item cannot finish yet. The items list, board, inbox, chat cards, contact and team pages, sessions, stats and Bot pages all show this same state, so an item whose move is yours is under 等你 everywhere. A work graph from an earlier cycle no longer changes the item's state.
- On the item page, a long goal folds to its first three lines. Runs of process notes fold to one line without Markdown marks. The activity is grouped under day headings with the clock time on each line. Progress and properties sit in a side column, with rarely used properties folded.
- Collaboration on the item page is one list with a row per step: its Bot and model, what it is doing now, on which try it passed, why it failed or what it asks you, a progress bar, and its working time and cost, earlier tries included. Each row has one action: 去处理 (decide), 重试 (retry) or 打开 (open). Steps that need you, failed or are running always show. Of the finished steps only the latest three show, the rest behind "显示其余 N 步". The header counts what needs you, failed, runs and is done, and shows the elapsed time against the agents' working time and the estimated cost. When some tokens ran on models without a price, it says so instead of showing a near-zero cost. Selecting a row opens its detail beside the page, with its earlier tries.
- "查看全图" opens the whole collaboration as one dependency graph of its steps. Each card shows the step's Bot, title, outcome, tries, working time and cost. Steps that could run at the same time share a column. Select a step for its goal and report, outputs, checks, failure reason, earlier tries, neighbouring steps, retry or rework, and, on request, its tool calls on a timeline.
- The items page has three views: list, board and the new 协作 view. In 协作, each open item that coordinates steps is a card, and its steps hang beside it with their state, cost and time. The 关系图 view of all items is gone, because it grew into a tangle. An open item's row now names what it waits for ("等 ST-28"), and an item's own relation graph still opens from its menu.
- The board has four columns, sorted the way the list groups items: "Your move", "In progress", "Paused / blocked" and "Done". An empty column keeps its place and says so. Each card shows the number, the title, its state in words and plan progress. Its arrow opens the item, and clicking the card unfolds its latest steps. Done shows the latest eight until "Show all". The counts under the title, the filters, the board and the running-status pill always agree.
- Items closed as failed have their own Failed group in the list, count as closed on project pages, read Failed on conversation cards and can be reopened.
- Item goals that Steve writes, and the briefs a coordinator writes for its steps, are short Markdown: one sentence on what will be done and what counts as done, then only the sections that apply.
- In 待处理 (Inbox), the 已处理 tab reads back the last 7 days by day and by item. Open an item to see each decision, who made it and, while possible, an undo. Filters separate your decisions, the ones YOLO made for you and the ones that lapsed. An item whose collaboration cannot run (its Node is unavailable, a Bot is off) counts as waiting on you. It is listed under 无法继续 with the reason, and the item states that reason instead of "协作已暂停".
- A work graph that its coordinator paused without asking you anything now waits for you: Steve opens a card with the coordinator's reason, and the item moves to Waiting for you instead of sitting under Paused. A graph you paused yourself stays quiet.

- Every memory on the Knowledge page says what it is about and where it came from, and both are links. The subject opens its view. The source opens the day of the main conversation, the item, the agent session, the mail thread or the event it was learned from, and hovering over it shows who noted it and the words it was based on. Each memory also says how many times Steve has used it. Memories in To confirm link to their sources the same way.
- The Long-term memory and Knowledge entry tabs are memory panels. They group memories by kind with filter chips. You can reword a memory in place, mark it stale (kept but no longer used) or use it again, and delete it with a few seconds to undo. Closing or reloading the page during those seconds still deletes it. A new memory picks its kind (rule or preference, fact, event or summary) in the panel. Memories show in English too, and memories you wrote say "Written by you" in your language.
- A new Subjects tab lists the people, projects and Bots Steve knows something about, with how many memories, documents and unconfirmed memories each has. Opening one shows what Steve knows and where it learned it, and the sessions, answers and searches that used it lately. A project also gathers its item records and the memories its deliveries left.
- The Sources tab shows each document source's state (OK, syncing, error or paused). A new section, Where memories come from, counts who writes memories (you, the main conversation, item deliveries, agent sessions, a Bot, mail, plugins) and how many still wait for you. It also says whether search finds things by meaning with OpenViking or uses the built-in keyword index.
- A new Tidy-up tab runs a knowledge steward on a schedule (daily, weekly or monthly), or at once. The steward merges memories that say the same thing, settles contradictions (the newest explicit correction wins), marks outdated ones stale, and fills in who a memory is about or which item it came from. It is an ordinary Bot made from the new Knowledge steward template, on an ordinary schedule, so its rules are on its Bot page and its time under Automation › Schedules.
- Each tidy-up run lists every change with its reason. A merge waits for you in To confirm. A conflict the steward could not settle asks which memory to keep. Every other change has Undo, and an answered conflict can be reopened.
- Agents on every harness have two new tools: `steve_memory_list` lists memories with their sources and use counts, and `steve_memory_tidy` makes the changes above. Changes any agent makes show up in the Tidy-up tab under the item they came from. Migration 64 records these changes and the merges.

- With the interface in English, text that the Hub writes from its own fixed wording now reads in English. This covers provider and connection names (Feishu / Lark, Other chat app, GitHub personal token, Other mailbox), their capability notes, permission purposes and form fields, GitHub event types, the built-in price table's notes, the built-in knowledge sources, memory candidate cards, why a card closed by itself, prerequisite-wait reasons, YOLO hard limits, the status line of an item whose work the Hub is judging (paused, queued, failed, waiting for prerequisites, a Node or a model quota) and the Hub's notifications. Built-in delivery tags, item category descriptions, a device's runner role and tool permission questions ("Allow running: …") are in English too.
- In English, what a new installation sets up in Chinese reads in English until you change it: the workspace name (Personal), the main Bot's role, speaking style and rules, the default channel persona and the first-meeting welcome. Saving a form unchanged keeps the original Chinese text, and anything you wrote stays as written. Bot names stay as they are, since `@` matches them.
- Chinese is unchanged, and what people and models wrote is always shown as written. Screen readers hear key names, field buttons (show, clear, copy), spinners and other control labels in your language.

- The web client has a new look. It uses Geist with the system CJK face, one accent colour on cool neutrals, a fixed type and radius scale, soft elevation instead of nested frames, and one page frame, so titles line up on every page. Motion follows one scale: 120 ms for colour and press, 180 ms for menus, toggles and page changes, 260 ms for dialogs, sheets and anything that opens or folds.
- Buttons, view switchers, filter chips, checkboxes, switches, text fields, selects, search boxes, badges, spinners, skeletons, cards and empty states look and behave the same on every page, in light and dark mode. Selects take type-ahead and never get cut off. Date pickers are small calendars in your language. Number fields have − and + buttons and stay within their limits.
- Dialogs, confirmations and side sheets share one look. On a phone or in a narrow window they slide up from the bottom across the full width. Menus have one soft highlight that follows the pointer and the arrow keys. Tooltips show after a short rest of the pointer, or at once under keyboard focus. Toasts stack, can be swiped away and appear just above the composer, so they no longer cover Send or Stop; F8 moves the focus to the newest.
- The sidebar is a flat app shell. At the top are your workspace with its menu and a button that folds the sidebar to a rail of icons (⌘B still works). Below come the Hub, the YOLO line, a running-status pill ("1 in progress · 2 waiting on you", which opens the open items with their current step and lets you pause, resume or open them) and a search field. At the bottom is your account: name, role in this workspace, and a menu with 我, Devices, Accounts, Settings and Sign out. In a short window the lower groups scroll between the header and the footer.
- Pages under another page (an item, a device, a project, a contact) show a breadcrumb in their top bar. On a phone the tab bar takes the sidebar's colour.
- New names: 接入 is now 连接 (Connections), 渠道 is now 群聊与私聊 (Chats), and the generic connector for chat apps other than Feishu, formerly 外部渠道 (External channel), is now 其他聊天工具 (Other chat app). **Capabilities** (能力) is a new entry next to Bots.

Once you type, it finds items and sessions by title or number, sessions whose transcript mentions the text, messages of the main conversation (selecting one jumps to it), and contacts and teams. - The first-run tour in the main conversation is one card in four steps: a hello, the runtimes found on this machine, the projects of the current space, and the first meeting (for the instance's owner) or a first task (for anyone else). - Many pages read better on a phone: tabs move below the title, contact details and option values wrap instead of running past the edge, and lists reach the right edge of the page. Rows that hold their own buttons no longer put a button inside a button.
- The component showroom at `#/design/components` shows every component in its states, in both themes. Some components come from affectUI Pro, which is licensed separately from Steve. Each such source file says so on its first line.

- A project's page has two tabs, Overview and Settings, and the description sits under the project's name. The overview leads with:

  - **Last 7 days**: items done, changes merged and versions released (for a git project), failures by kind, estimated cost, agent time and the time items waited on you. "Open in statistics" opens the stats page for this project. This replaces the "last 30 days" line in the header.
  - **Repository and delivery**: one line each for the folder and machine, the repository and branch, the linked GitHub repository, how changes merge and how they are released. - People can have a photo. On your own page (我) or a contact's page, click the avatar to upload a PNG, JPEG or WebP image up to 10 MB.
