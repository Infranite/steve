- When Steve drafts a chat reply, it now reads the conversation the way you do. Your own lines show as `<你的名字>（我）` and what Steve sent for you as `（我，经 Steve 发出）`. Steve tells your lines apart by your account and contact, not by name, so an account whose display name is a number still reads as you. All your names are listed once, and @-ing any of them means someone is looking for you. Numeric ids and raw handles never appear as names.
- Private chats and groups are both read in stretches of talk: the stretch the message belongs to and the one before it. A gap of half an hour starts a new stretch, and the gap shows. Every line has a relative time ("3 分钟前", "昨天 21:40"). A line Steve sent for you is placed at the time it was sent, not when it was drafted, so a draft you approved later sits where it appeared in the chat. Lines in the same Feishu reply chain as the message are marked, and earlier lines of that chain are added when they fall outside the stretches.
- A new 【场合】 block tells the composer whether this is a private chat or a group (with its member count and whether outsiders are in it), why the message reached Steve (a private chat, an @, a reply to you, your trigger words), and the local time. Messages that arrived after the one being answered are shown and marked, so Steve does not answer a question that has already moved on. Drafts still waiting in the chat are listed so they are not written twice.
- Runs of bot messages fold into one line. Placeholders such as `[unsupported message]`, `[system message]` or `[sticker]` become plain descriptions. A message with only an attachment is kept. When Steve has read an image in a message, a one-line description is kept with it, and later conversations show that description instead of "unread". Steve is told never to guess what is in an image, card or voice message it could not read.
- Your own account takes your name when you speak, if its display name was an id (from another account of yours, else your contact's name).
- Feishu rich text keeps the text of tags Steve does not know, instead of dropping them. On an external chat connection, a message whose text is only a placeholder is read again from the Feishu message in its `raw` payload, when that payload carries one, so rich text and cards keep their words.
- On your own account, a message nobody can read (a voice message, forwarded chat history, a card) that is still not understood after the wider read is recorded, not asked about: you see it in the chat yourself. On a bot it still comes to you as a question, because whoever asked the bot may be in a chat you are not in.
