- The collaboration on the item page is now drawn as lanes, one per Bot: the coordinator's Bot first, then every Bot in the order the work reached it. Each step, earlier try and forked item its Bot ran is a block on one time axis; a step that picked up work from another is joined to it by a thin curve. Running work is blue and reaches a "now" line, work waiting for your decision is amber with a "Needs you" flag, failed work is red, and stopped work and earlier tries are hatched or dimmed. The window is the time the work actually took, so an earlier round spanning hours shows those hours rather than the last half hour, and it only follows the clock while something runs. A Bot gets another row only for work it really ran at the same time, and the coordinator's whole span lies under its other blocks, so its lane stays one row. Even a run of a few seconds is a block wide enough to see and tap; one that would cover the block before it moves right next to it, so a quick failed retry at the end is never hidden under the try before it. A block too short for its title shows the title beside it where its row has room (in the block's colour, with the "Needs you" flag), and its tooltip always has it. Under the lanes every step is listed with its title, Bot, how it ended (for example "passed on try 4"), its state and its working time, including steps that have not started yet or have no time on record, which say what they wait for. The header keeps the coordinator, steps, agents, elapsed time against agent working time, cost, and the failure and decision pills. Selecting a block still opens its detail beside the page (an earlier try opens its step, with all tries listed); while the detail is open the lanes make room so every block stays reachable. On phones the lanes scroll sideways, each lane is named above its blocks, and when nothing is running the lanes open at the latest failed or waiting work.
- The lanes are built on the Agent Lanes component from affectUI Pro, which is licensed separately from Steve; its source file says so on its first line.
